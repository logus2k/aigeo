# New Zealand — AI Strategy: Investing with Confidence (July 2025)

**Source:** [new_zealand_ai_strategy_2025.pdf](new_zealand_ai_strategy_2025.pdf), 19 pages. Published July 2025 by the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment (MBIE), Hīkina Whakatutuki. ISBN (online) 978-1-991316-78-3. Foreword signed by Hon Dr Shane Reti, Minister of Science, Innovation & Technology. The strategy explicitly states it was drafted with AI assistance under human supervision, in line with the Government Chief Digital Officer's *Responsible AI Guidance for the Public Service: GenAI* and the Algorithm Charter for Aotearoa.

## Document type

A **private-sector AI uptake strategy**, not a horizontal national plan in the Portuguese ANIA or Brazilian PBIA sense. The strategy is explicitly subtitled "Accelerating Private Sector AI Adoption and Innovation" [p.1]. A separate work programme on AI in the Public Service is driven by Hon Judith Collins KC, Minister for Digitising Government [p.2]. New Zealand was the **only OECD country that had not published an AI strategy** before this document [p.2] — the strategy itself frames late publication as a deliberate choice, prioritising adoption over development.

## Vision and framing — "Invest with Confidence" [p.2-3]

- Core message: New Zealand businesses can **invest in AI with confidence**; government commits to reducing uncertainty, removing unintended barriers, and providing clear regulatory guidance within the existing legal framework.
- The strategy positions NZ as a **smart adopter**, not a foundational developer. Competing with Google, OpenAI or other model developers is rejected as economically irrational; advantage lies in being sophisticated adopters that **identify, adapt and deploy AI solutions to local challenges** [p.9].
- Economic anchor: Microsoft (2024) industry research estimating generative AI could add **NZ$76 billion to the NZ economy by 2038**, or over 15% of GDP [p.2].
- Sits inside the Government's flagship economic approach, **Going for Growth** [p.2].

## Structure — 4 barriers × 4 actions [p.10-12]

The strategy's central operational architecture is a barrier-action matrix rather than a pillar structure:

| Barrier | Government action |
|---|---|
| **Regulatory uncertainty** | Light-touch, principles-based approach. Existing frameworks (privacy, consumer protection, human rights) are technology-neutral and remain. NZ adopts the **OECD AI Principles** as the governance frame; no domestic AI Act is proposed [p.10] |
| **Perceived complexity, ethics, risk** | Companion document *Responsible AI Guidance for Businesses* (MBIE), covering cybersecurity, privacy, IP, bias management [p.11] |
| **Lack of understanding and perceived value** | Publication of this Strategy itself; awareness-building; framing AI as augmentation rather than displacement [p.11] |
| **Lack of AI skills** | Budget 2025 allocations: **NZ$213M tuition and training subsidies, NZ$64M STEM and priority areas, NZ$111M enrolments and Youth Guarantee** [p.12]. University programmes (University of Auckland Software Engineering, Victoria University of Wellington Master of AI). MBIE supporting Business Mentors NZ and Regional Business Partner Network (>5,000 businesses/year) [p.12]. AI masterclasses for public-service leaders via the Government Chief Digital Officer |

## Quantitative indicators [p.6, 11, 12, 15, 16]

- Larger NZ businesses using AI: **67% (2024), up from 48% (2023)** (Datacom)
- SMEs with no plans to adopt AI: **68%** (Spark/NZIER 2024); compared to **38%** in Australia
- Workers who have heard of AI: **97%**; who can explain it clearly: **34%** (Verian 2024)
- Non-users citing skills shortage as primary barrier: **43%** (Datacom)
- AI-related RDTI (R&D Tax Incentive) approved spend since 2019: **NZ$611M**
- SI&T annual public investment: **NZ$1.2B**
- Robinson Research Institute advanced-technology platform: **NZ$71M**
- Singapore-NZ AI for Healthy Ageing Initiative: **NZ$12M joint research**
- Catalyst Fund 2024-2028: AI named as **one of six research priority areas**

## Governance and instruments [p.3, 10, 13-16]

- **OECD AI Principles** (5 values-based: inclusive growth, human rights and democratic values, transparency, robustness, accountability) treated as the operative normative framework — adopted **without additional regulatory overlay** [p.10].
- **Algorithm Charter for Aotearoa**: pre-existing commitment to fair, ethical, transparent use of algorithms by government [p.iv].
- **Centre for Data Ethics and Innovation**: published guidance on Māori data [p.13].
- **Te Puni Kōkiri**: exploring support for appropriate use of cultural IP and prevention of commercial misappropriation [p.13].
- **Invest New Zealand**: new entity to attract FDI in AI and accelerate decisions [p.14].
- **Overseas Investment Act 2005** reform: speed up decisions while protecting national interests [p.14].
- **Active Investor Plus Visa** changes from 1 April 2025: simplified categories, expanded acceptable investments [p.14].
- **Going to chair the 2027 Digital Nations meeting** [p.14].

## What New Zealand does that Portugal's ANIA does NOT do

1. **Treaty of Waitangi obligations embedded in the strategy.** Māori communities, mātauranga Māori, and Māori data are treated as **taonga** (treasured cultural property); appropriate use of cultural IP is a strategic concern, with Te Puni Kōkiri exploring concrete protections [p.13]. Portugal's ANIA has no analogous indigenous-rights anchor or cultural-IP regime.
2. **Light-touch, principles-based regulatory posture by design.** NZ explicitly rejects an AI-Act-equivalent domestic overlay [p.10]. Portugal's Responsibility and Ethics pillar engages with AI Act compliance as a substantive duty.
3. **Adoption-only strategic posture.** Foundational model development is explicitly excluded as outside NZ's economic capacity [p.9]. Portugal pursues both adoption (II.5-II.11) and sovereign-model ambition (AMALIA).
4. **Strategy itself written with AI under human oversight, with transparency on the methodology** [p.iv, p.13]. Portugal's ANIA has no equivalent declarative-transparency gesture.
5. **Indigenous-language AI use cases as national exemplars.** Te Hiku Media's te reo Māori automatic speech recognition (92% accuracy) and Kaituhi bilingual transcription service [p.7]; KIWA Digital supporting 900+ language codes through VoiceQ/CultureQ. Portugal's AMALIA addresses Portuguese but has no analogous indigenous-language deployment scale.
6. **Sector adoption case studies named in the strategy text.** Halter (agritech unicorn at USD 1bn), Toku (eye exam scans), Formus Labs (orthopaedic planning), Alimetry (gastric care), NIWA × Kaikōura whale watching, Lynker Analytics (Land Information NZ) [p.6-8]. Portugal's ANIA cites sectoral applications generically without named-company case studies.
7. **Bilateral international research initiative with named partner and budget.** Singapore-NZ AI for Healthy Ageing, NZ$12M [p.14]. Portugal's IV.7 technology-diplomacy track has no equivalent named bilateral instrument.
8. **OECD AI Principles treated as the sufficient governance frame.** No domestic AI-specific legislation is contemplated. Portugal sits under the EU AI Act regime; the comparison reveals an alternative regulatory model.

## What Portugal does that New Zealand does NOT do

1. **Horizontal pillar structure with 32 named initiatives** [Portugal ANIA p.6 onward]. NZ's 4 barriers + 4 actions are operationally narrower.
2. **Quantitative outcome KPIs at population scale.** Portugal: 1.3M reskilled, 320k displaced, €18-22B/yr GDP add, 8% GDP at peak. NZ provides input figures (Budget 2025 allocations) and adoption diagnostics but no aggregate outcome targets.
3. **Sovereign computing infrastructure (Deucalion, MareNostrum5 share).** NZ has no equivalent named national HPC.
4. **AI Centre of Excellence in Public Administration as a Pillar II flagship.** NZ has a separate Minister for Digitising Government, but no equivalent named institution.
5. **Sovereign LLM ambition (AMALIA).** NZ rejects foundational-model development entirely.
6. **EU AI Act implementation guide and risk-assessment toolkit** [Portugal ANIA Responsibility pillar]. NZ's framework is principles-based and does not specify compliance machinery.
7. **Sectoral AI centres (II.5).** NZ's sector engagement is innovation-case-study rather than institutional.

## Distinctive analytical note

New Zealand's strategy is **the smallest in the corpus by ambition** (19 pages, adoption-focused, no aggregate outcome KPIs) but is structurally distinctive on two axes: the **explicit integration of indigenous-rights obligations** into a national technology strategy, and the **deliberate non-adoption of AI-specific legislation** in favour of OECD-aligned principles plus updates to existing technology-neutral law. For ANIA comparison, the Waitangi-Māori dimension supplies an indigenous-rights template that has no Portuguese analogue, while the regulatory minimalism stands in deliberate contrast to the EU AI Act regime within which Portugal operates.
